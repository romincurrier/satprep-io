PSAT SCORE GUIDE

Use a PSAT score report to identify the next skills to strengthen.

The PSAT/NMSQT and PSAT 10 report a total score from 320 to 1520, made up of Reading and Writing and Math section scores from 160 to 760. Those numbers summarize performance, but the most useful study decisions usually come from looking beneath the total score at section and skill evidence.

Current PSAT/NMSQT and PSAT 10 score ranges

Total score

320–1520

The total combines the Reading and Writing and Math section scores.

Reading and Writing

160–760

This section covers Information and Ideas, Craft and Structure, Expression of Ideas, and Standard English Conventions.

Math

160–760

This section covers Algebra, Advanced Math, Problem-Solving and Data Analysis, and Geometry and Trigonometry.

The SAT Suite uses a common score scale, with different reported ranges

College Board describes the SAT Suite as using a common score scale across assessments. That means a section score represents the same level of academic achievement across the SAT Suite when the same numerical score appears. The assessments nevertheless report different possible ranges because they are designed for different grade-level contexts.

The SAT total range is 400–1600 with 200–800 section scores. The PSAT/NMSQT and PSAT 10 report 320–1520 totals with 160–760 section scores. So a PSAT score should not simply be converted by adding points to make it look like an SAT score; instead, use the common scale and the student's actual report to understand current performance and growth. For an overview of shared structure and differences, see the SAT vs. PSAT guide.

What else appears on a score report?

College Board score reports can include a score range and percentile information in addition to the total and section scores. The score range reflects expected variation if a student took a different administration under identical conditions. Percentiles provide a comparison with other students in the applicable reporting group.

Important: percentile values and reporting details can vary by assessment and reporting year. Use the current official College Board score report for the student's actual administration when interpreting those values.

Turn a score into a study plan

1. Start with section balance

Compare Reading and Writing with Math. A large difference can help identify where the first study time may have the greatest value.

2. Look for domain evidence

Domain-level performance can point toward areas such as Algebra, Information and Ideas, or Standard English Conventions rather than treating the section as one broad skill.

3. Confirm with fresh assessment

Prior scores are evidence, not the whole picture. A current diagnostic can confirm whether an older weak area still deserves priority.

4. Update from practice

As a student practices, newer performance should gradually outweigh older evidence and change what comes next.

How SATprep.io uses prior testing

SATprep.io can use supported prior testing reports as one input to a student's learning model. The initial diagnostic remains assessment-only so it measures current performance without teaching answers during the test. Learning and practice sessions then provide explanations and update mastery evidence over time.

That design avoids treating one PSAT score as a permanent label. The goal is to use the score report to make the next study decision more informed.
