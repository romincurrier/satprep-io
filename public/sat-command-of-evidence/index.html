SAT COMMAND OF EVIDENCE

Choose the evidence that proves the claim most directly.

College Board’s current Information and Ideas domain includes Command of Evidence questions using text and, in some cases, informational graphics. The task is not simply to find a related detail; it is to identify the evidence that most directly supports the specific claim or conclusion in the question.

A repeatable evidence-selection process

  1. Identify the claim. Put the exact conclusion you need to support into simple words.
  2. Separate evidence from background. A detail can be relevant to the topic without proving the claim.
  3. Compare answer choices by directness. Prefer evidence that establishes the requested relationship with the fewest assumptions.
  4. Match the strength of the claim. Evidence of an association does not automatically prove causation or universality.
  5. For data, verify the numbers. Read labels, units, groups, and direction of change before deciding what a graph or table supports.

Textual and quantitative evidence use the same core habit

Textual evidence

Look for a sentence or detail that directly establishes the claim. Avoid choices that require an extra story about motives, causes, or future outcomes.

Quantitative evidence

Find the numerical comparison that corresponds to the claim. Confirm categories, axes, percentages, and units before interpreting the difference.

Useful question: “If this were the only evidence I could show someone, would it actually justify the claim?”

Watch for causation traps

If a study observes that two variables move together, that may support an association. It does not necessarily establish that one variable caused the other. Strong evidence practice includes controlling the language of the conclusion: associated with can be justified when caused is not.

Evidence questions are not memory tests

The strongest preparation develops a habit of claim-to-evidence matching. Practice should vary the topic and representation so the student learns to evaluate evidence itself rather than remember a familiar passage.

SATprep.io approach: practice explanations identify why the correct detail or data comparison supports the claim and why stronger or less direct choices fail. Diagnostic questions remain feedback-free while the baseline is being measured.

Use evidence results as a distinct learning signal

A student may understand a passage but still choose weak evidence, or read a graph correctly but overstate its conclusion. SATprep.io tracks these narrower signals so the learning path can target the reasoning problem instead of repeating all Reading & Writing content.
