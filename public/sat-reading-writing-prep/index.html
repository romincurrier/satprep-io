A repeatable approach beats vague “reading practice”

  1. Name the task. Is the question asking for an inference, evidence, a word meaning, a transition, punctuation, or another specific skill?
  2. Find the controlling evidence or rule. Use passage details for reading questions and grammatical structure for conventions questions.
  3. Eliminate answers that overreach. Strong distractors often sound sophisticated but say more than the text or rule allows.
  4. Review the reasoning after practice. Correct/incorrect feedback matters most when the student understands why the answer works.
  5. Revisit weak skills. Repeated misses in one skill should change what the student studies next.

How much time is available?

College Board currently gives students 64 minutes for 54 Reading and Writing questions, split into two 32-minute modules. Because the section contains many short, discrete tasks, efficient recognition of the question type can matter as much as reading speed.
