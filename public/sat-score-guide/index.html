SAT SCORE GUIDE

Understand your SAT score, then use it to decide what to study next.

The SAT reports a total score from 400 to 1600. That total is the sum of a Reading and Writing section score and a Math section score, each reported from 200 to 800. The total is useful, but section and skill evidence usually tell you more about what to work on next.

Current SAT score ranges

Total score

400–1600

The total combines Reading and Writing with Math.

Reading and Writing

200–800

This section measures skills across Information and Ideas, Craft and Structure, Expression of Ideas, and Standard English Conventions.

Math

200–800

This section measures Algebra, Advanced Math, Problem-Solving and Data Analysis, and Geometry and Trigonometry.

What does the score range on a report mean?

College Board score reports also show a score range. The range reflects expected variation if the same student took a different administration under identical conditions. It is a reminder that a reported score is an estimate of performance, not a perfectly fixed measurement.

That is one reason a study plan should not be built around a single number alone. A useful plan also considers the section split, domain evidence, recent practice, and the student's test date.

How should you use percentiles?

Percentiles compare a student's score with scores from an applicable comparison group. They can add context, but a percentile is not a mastery percentage. For study planning, it is more useful to ask which specific skills are producing missed questions than to treat the percentile as a list of topics already learned or not learned.

Keep the measurement types separate: a scaled SAT score, percentile, and skill-performance indicator describe different things. SATprep.io preserves those distinctions when prior testing is used as learning evidence.

Turn an SAT score report into a study plan

1. Compare the two sections

Start with the Reading and Writing and Math scores. A large gap can identify the section that deserves the first deeper review.

2. Find domain patterns

Within each section, look for patterns in areas such as Algebra, Advanced Math, Information and Ideas, or Standard English Conventions.

3. Confirm the weak areas

An older score report is evidence, not a permanent diagnosis. Use fresh assessment or practice to confirm whether the same gaps remain.

4. Let recent performance take over

As new practice evidence accumulates, the learning plan should adapt instead of continuing to rely on an old score indefinitely.

The SAT Suite uses a common score scale, with different reported ranges

College Board describes the SAT Suite as using a common score scale. When the same numerical section score appears across assessments, it represents the same level of academic achievement. The possible reporting ranges differ because the assessments are designed for different grade-level contexts.

The SAT reports a 400–1600 total with 200–800 section scores. The PSAT/NMSQT and PSAT 10 report 320–1520 totals with 160–760 section scores. That means a PSAT result should not be mechanically converted by adding points; the shared scale is more useful for monitoring growth while each assessment retains its own reported range.
